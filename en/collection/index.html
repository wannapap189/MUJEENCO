MUJEEN · 01

Four-Eared, Five-Eyed Deity

Four ears, five eyes, a distinctive form.

Through the eyes of MUJEEN, the Four-Eared, Five-Eyed Deity brings belief into contemporary form. Vivid colour and an iridescent glaze reveal changing reflections from every angle.

The materials within

Beneath the circular front lid is a compartment for materials prepared through Maoshan Taoist feng shui rituals, connecting the design with its devotional tradition.

Auspicious qualities · In devotional belief

Competition and negotiation.

The Four-Eared, Five-Eyed Deity is associated with beliefs in good fortune and negotiation. Its curled tail recalls the story of Angada’s negotiation. MUJEEN expresses these meanings through contemporary sacred art, inviting a closer understanding of belief through form.

Details of the artwork
  • Four ears, five eyes and three brown hair forms
  • Inscriptions on the garment, hands and soles
  • Rear inscriptions and a curled tail on the base

MUJEEN · 02

The Gambling Immortal

A stately presence in red and gold.

The Gambling Immortal sits on a rectangular base, wearing a gold hat and red robes edged in green. Gold motifs continue around the figure.

Auspicious qualities · In devotional belief

Fortune and trade.

The Gambling Immortal is associated with devotional beliefs in supporting and strengthening one’s fortune. MUJEEN gives this tradition a stately contemporary form and invites an understanding of the deity’s meaning and devotional practice before bringing it home.

Details of the artwork
  • Gold hat and green-edged red robes
  • Green tablet and gold object in the hands
  • Rectangular base with line-and-dot patterns

MUJEEN · 03

Kuman Fa Cai

Palms together, framed in red and gold.

Kuman Fa Cai sits with palms together, framed by red and gold ornament. An iridescent finish invites the eye to travel from the gentle expression to the details of the base.

Auspicious qualities · In devotional belief

Wisdom and working life.

Kuman Fa Cai is a divine child associated with beliefs in good fortune. Through joined palms, red and gold ornament, and an iridescent finish, MUJEEN brings this devotional story into a contemporary work that rewards a closer look.

Details of the artwork
  • Palms together, with red and gold ornament
  • Cards, dice, chips and gold forms on a cloud-patterned base
  • Inscriptions on the back and lettering on the base

QUESTIONS & GUIDANCE

Before you begin.

How do I choose a devotional figure?

Begin with the beliefs and stories in the collection, explore the four views, then speak with MUJEEN about your chosen figure before making a decision.

Where can I ask about the talismans and inscriptions?

Contact MUJEEN on LINE for the meanings of individual inscriptions, talismans and details, as well as guidance on devotional practice.

What is the story behind a MUJEEN figure?

MUJEEN is a contemporary devotional art brand with its own collection of devotional figures. Ajarn Khaek Muntra Siam conducts the rituals and consecration according to Maoshan Taoist feng shui teachings. After devotional materials are placed inside, he personally inscribes talismans and sacred characters onto each figure again.
